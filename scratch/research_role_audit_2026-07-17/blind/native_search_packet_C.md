## Factual research summary

### Election mechanics and timing

- Zambia’s first-round presidential ballot is scheduled for **August 13, 2026**. [A: official] [Electoral Commission of Zambia](https://www.elections.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/2026-Elections-Timetable-14-August-2025-30-days.pdf)
- A candidate must receive **more than 50% of valid votes cast** to win in the first round. Otherwise, the two leading candidates proceed to a second ballot within 37 days. [A: official] [Electoral Commission of Zambia](https://www.elections.org.zm/?page_id=1884)
- Hichilema formally filed for re-election with Vice President Mutale Nalumango again serving as his running mate. [A: official] [Zambia News and Information Services](https://www.zanis.gov.zm/?p=4239)

### Most informative polling evidence

- The strongest publicly available survey located is the **Zambia Election Research Network’s first-round survey**, conducted from **June 24 to November 15, 2025**. It found:
  - **55%** planned to vote for Hichilema;
  - **13%** planned to vote for an opposition party;
  - **32%** did not know or refused to answer.  
  [A: primary academic research] [University of Cape Town—Centre for Social Science Research](https://humanities.uct.ac.za/cssr/idcppa/publication/bp-03-26)

- This was a stratified random-digit-dial telephone survey intended to meet nationally representative quotas. Important limitations are that it covered a nearly six-month fieldwork period, represented only people accessible by mobile phone, and was completed before Brian Mundubile emerged as the consolidated principal challenger. [A: primary academic research] [ZERN methodology paper](https://saipar.org/assets/files/1781797877_idcppa-briefing-beardsworthkabingaseekingswahman.pdf)

- Reuters’ July 9 assessment consequently described Hichilema as favored but warned that Mundubile could mount a strong challenge. University of Zambia political scientist Lee Habasonda told Reuters that the opposition began organizing late but “should not be dismissed.” [B: Reuters] [Reuters via MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/zambia-election-will-put-hichilema-s-economic-record-to-the-test-ce7f5ed9d18cf723)

- A separate poll reported in late 2025 placed Hichilema at approximately **60% nationally**, with opposition candidates collectively at 35%, and reportedly put him between **58% and 63%** in hypothetical head-to-head contests. However, accessible reports provide less methodological information than the ZERN study, so this evidence should receive less weight. [C: local-media report] [Open Zambia](https://www.openzambia.com/politics/2026/3/5/hh-wins-poll-on-mwambas-page-with-majority-of-votes)

- Social-media and call-in polls are highly inconsistent and are not representative samples. For example, a March EMV exercise gave Hichilema **51.2% in its Facebook poll**, but only **11.3% in online voting** and **4.4% among live callers**. These divergent results primarily demonstrate selection effects rather than reliable national voting intentions. [C: local-media/opt-in polling] [Zambian Observer](https://zambianobserver.com/final-results-on-emv-opinion-poll/)

### Historical reference class

Hichilema’s vote share has risen substantially in his two most recent presidential contests:

- **2016:** 1,760,347 votes, or approximately **47.63%**, narrowly losing to Edgar Lungu. [A: official] [Electoral Commission of Zambia](https://www.elections.org.zm/?p=2881)
- **2021:** 2,852,348 votes, equal to **57.51% of all ballots cast** in the ECZ table; this corresponds to roughly 59% when calculated against valid candidate votes alone. [A: official] [Electoral Commission of Zambia](https://www.elections.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2021-PresidentialResultsPerConst.pdf)

Thus, in the two previous elections held under Zambia’s majority/runoff system, Hichilema exceeded 50% once out of two contests. His 2021 share was roughly ten percentage points above his 2016 result. [A: official] [ECZ historical-results portal](https://www.elections.org.zm/?page_id=4293)

### Factors supporting Hichilema’s vote share

- Reuters identifies incumbency—including access to state resources and easier nationwide transportation—as a material organizational advantage. [B: Reuters] [Reuters via MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/zambia-election-will-put-hichilema-s-economic-record-to-the-test-ce7f5ed9d18cf723)
- Annual inflation reportedly fell to **6.5% in June 2026**, its lowest level in more than eight years, following debt restructuring and an economic rebound supported by copper prices. [B: Reuters] [Reuters via MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/zambia-election-will-put-hichilema-s-economic-record-to-the-test-ce7f5ed9d18cf723)
- Hichilema’s campaign points to debt restructuring, free education, public-sector teacher and health-worker recruitment, improved foreign-exchange reserves and renewed mining investment. These are campaign/government claims rather than independent measures of electoral support. [C: paper of record reporting party claims] [Mail & Guardian](https://mg.co.za/news/africa/2026-07-16-two-horse-race-to-zambian-august-poll/)

### Factors that could reduce his share

- Despite improving macroeconomic indicators, Reuters reports that many households still feel squeezed by living costs, giving Mundubile an opening to argue that aggregate gains have not improved ordinary living standards. [B: Reuters] [Reuters via MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/zambia-election-will-put-hichilemas-economic-record-to-the-test-ce7f5ed9d18cf723)
- In the ZERN survey, views of personal economic circumstances were divided: **41% said conditions had worsened during the preceding year and 38% said they had improved**. [A: primary academic research] [University of Cape Town—CSSR](https://humanities.uct.ac.za/cssr/idcppa/publication/bp-03-26)
- Mundubile entered the race after much of the ZERN polling had already been completed, meaning the most credible published poll does not fully measure the current two-candidate dynamic. [B: Reuters] [Reuters via MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/zambia-election-will-put-hichilemas-economic-record-to-the-test-ce7f5ed9d18cf723)
- Opposition parties and civil-society organizations have criticized restrictions on campaigning, a 2025 cybercrime law and constitutional changes expanding parliament; Hichilema denies suppressing political dissent. These controversies may affect both support and perceptions of electoral fairness. [B: Reuters] [Reuters via MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/zambia-election-will-put-hichilemas-economic-record-to-the-test-ce7f5ed9d18cf723)

### Prediction markets

- Polymarket’s election-winner contract recently assigned Hichilema approximately an **81–85% probability of ultimately winning**, with roughly **$55,000–$72,000** traded depending on the observation date. This market includes a possible runoff and therefore does **not** directly predict his first-round percentage. [C: prediction market] [Polymarket](https://polymarket.com/tl/event/zambia-presidential-election-winner-20260604205247322/will-hakainde-hichilema-win-the-2026-zambian-presidential-elections-20260604205342549)
- I did **not** find a reliable or liquid prediction-market contract specifically covering Hichilema’s first-round vote share.

### Bottom line for a forecaster

The best-supported quantitative benchmarks are **55% in the ZERN survey**, approximately **60% in a less-transparent late-2025 poll**, and Hichilema’s **57.51% of all ballots cast in 2021**. The main uncertainty is that the strongest survey predates Mundubile’s campaign and contains a very large **32% undecided/refusal group**. Reliable public evidence published close to election day remains limited; no high-quality July 2026 national vote-intention poll with full methodology was located.
