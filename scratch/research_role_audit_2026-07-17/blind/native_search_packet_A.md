## Research summary — Zambia 2026 presidential first round

**No election result exists yet:** Zambia’s first-round presidential vote is scheduled for **13 August 2026**; the Electoral Commission of Zambia (ECZ) timetable provides for results to be declared on **17 August**. [A: official] [ECZ, 2026 General Election Roadmap](https://www.elections.org.zm/wp-content/uploads/2026-Elections-Timetable-14-August-2025-30-days.pdf)

**Most directly relevant public vote-intention evidence:** A March 2026 briefing on the Zambia Election Research Network’s (ZERN) first survey wave reports that **55% of all weighted respondents said they planned to vote for the president/UPND candidate**, **13%** for an opposition party, and **32%** said “don’t know” or refused. The authors describe UPND as leading with 55% and PF at 10%, but explicitly caution that the large uncommitted/nonresponse group creates substantial uncertainty. [ZERN/University of Cape Town briefing](https://humanities.uct.ac.za/sites/default/files/media/documents/humanities_uct_ac_za/1380/idcppa-briefing-beardsworthkabingaseekingswahman.pdf) [university research]

- The survey was a weighted, stratified random-digit-dial telephone study of **1,497** people, fielded from **24 June–15 November 2025**. It was designed to approximate national population totals by region, urban/rural residence, and gender. [ZERN/University of Cape Town briefing](https://humanities.uct.ac.za/sites/default/files/media/documents/humanities_uct_ac_za/1380/idcppa-briefing-beardsworthkabingaseekingswahman.pdf) [university research]
- Important limitations reported by its authors: it is a phone survey and therefore represents people with phone access; its achieved sample was more urban, younger, and more educated than a typical face-to-face sample; and the reported 55% was collected over a nearly six-month **pre-campaign** period rather than at a single election-date snapshot. [ZERN/University of Cape Town briefing](https://humanities.uct.ac.za/sites/default/files/media/documents/humanities_uct_ac_za/1380/idcppa-briefing-beardsworthkabingaseekingswahman.pdf) [university research]
- The authors’ qualitative assessment was that UPND appeared headed toward a “relatively comfortable re-election,” while stressing that undecided/refusing respondents and turnout could alter the result. This is an expert interpretation, not an official forecast. [ZERN/University of Cape Town briefing](https://humanities.uct.ac.za/sites/default/files/media/documents/humanities_uct_ac_za/1380/idcppa-briefing-beardsworthkabingaseekingswahman.pdf) [university research]

### Incumbent-performance indicators

- In the same ZERN survey, **79%** of the weighted sample said they approved or strongly approved of Hichilema’s performance. Government ratings were comparatively weak on electricity (**27%** positive) and the cost of living (**44%** positive); views on the economy were close to evenly divided, with **38%** saying it had improved and **41%** saying it had worsened over the preceding year. [ZERN/University of Cape Town briefing](https://humanities.uct.ac.za/sites/default/files/media/documents/humanities_uct_ac_za/1380/idcppa-briefing-beardsworthkabingaseekingswahman.pdf) [university research]
- An earlier, independent Afrobarometer face-to-face survey, fielded **8–28 July 2024** with a national probability sample of **1,200** and a stated ±3 percentage-point margin of error, found **57.3%** approving or strongly approving of Hichilema and **38.0%** disapproving or strongly disapproving. It also found **66.2%** saying Zambia was going in the wrong direction. These are approval and national-direction measures—not vote-choice polling—and predate the election by more than two years. [Afrobarometer Zambia Round 10 results](https://www.afrobarometer.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Summary-of-results-Zambia-Afrobarometer-R10-bh-21feb25-.pdf) [A: primary survey]

### Candidate field and electoral rules

- Hichilema and Vice President Mutale Nalumango were formally declared validly nominated by the ECZ returning officer in May 2026. [A: official] [Zambia News and Information Services](https://www.zanis.gov.zm/?p=4239)
- Press reporting based on the nomination process stated that **14 presidential candidates** successfully filed nominations, including Hichilema, Harry Kalaba, Fred M’membe, Kelvin Fube Bwalya, and Brian Mundubile. A fragmented field can affect the incumbent’s first-round share even if the principal contest remains one-sided. [C: local outlet] [Lusaka Times](https://www.lusakatimes.com/2026/05/22/ecz-closes-presidential-nominations-with-14-candidates-on-ballot/)
- Zambia requires a candidate to receive **more than 50% of valid votes** to win in the first ballot. If no one does, the top two go to a runoff within 37 days. Thus, for this question, the pivotal threshold is **50%**, not merely a plurality. [A: official] [National Assembly of Zambia](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/node/6264)

### Prediction-market and low-quality polling evidence

- A Polymarket contract quoted by its public event page had Hichilema at **81% probability of winning** when crawled in late June 2026. This is a market-implied probability of victory, **not a forecast of his vote percentage**, and the page’s claim that polling averages show 55–63% support is not accompanied there by underlying poll documentation. [C: prediction-market page] [Polymarket](https://polymarket.com/pl/event/zambia-presidential-election-winner-20260604205247322/will-hakainde-hichilema-win-the-2026-zambian-presidential-elections-20260604205342549)
- I found a March 2026 “EMV opinion poll” published by the *Zambian Observer* showing Hichilema at **51.2% in a Facebook poll**, but it gives no sampling frame, sample size, field dates, weighting, or methodology. Its telephone-call and online-voting sections gave sharply different results. It should not be treated as representative evidence. [C: local outlet / unverified methodology] [Zambian Observer](https://zambianobserver.com/final-results-on-emv-opinion-poll/)

## Bottom line for a forecaster

The best-documented quantitative anchor is the **55% UPND/presidential vote-intention figure** in the ZERN Round 1 survey. It is above the **50%+1** first-round threshold but is not a clean election-day Hichilema vote-share estimate because **32%** of respondents were undecided or declined to state a choice, the survey predates the campaign and final field, and its authors disclose phone-sample limitations. No reliable, methodologically documented, post-nomination national poll with a direct Hichilema first-round vote-share estimate was found in this search.
