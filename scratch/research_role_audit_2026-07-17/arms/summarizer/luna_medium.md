## Scope and resolution

- The question opened on **2026-06-30**. Events dated before that date are **[PRE-WINDOW — occurred before question open, cannot itself satisfy the criteria]**, but may inform the historical base rate.
- The election is scheduled for **August 13, 2026**. Resolution is based on the percentage of votes Hakainde Hichilema receives in the first round, according to official results published by the Electoral Commission of Zambia (ECZ).

# Historical Context and Background

## 1. Previous election and incumbent status

- Hakainde Hichilema has been president since **2021** and is seeking a second term in the **August 13, 2026** election. [C: single-outlet reports; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- Hichilema defeated the late Edgar Lungu in the **2021** election. [C: single-outlet reports; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- The election is repeatedly characterized by analysts and media reports as a referendum on Hichilema’s economic record. [B/C: Reuters-based reporting republished by Afrimag, Africanews, and MarketScreener; **SINGLE-SOURCE as to the underlying Reuters report**]
- The Zambian presidential system is described as operating under a **“50+1 threshold”**, meaning a candidate must obtain more than half of valid votes to win in the first round. Former presidential aide Amos Chanda referred to this system when criticizing very high victory projections. [C: Zambia: News Diggers!, reporting Chanda’s remarks; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]

- The same survey reportedly found that **51%** of respondents expected the election to be free and fair. [B: Reuters, as reported by Afrimag/Africanews/MarketScreener; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- The survey was conducted before the question opened. **[PRE-WINDOW — occurred before question open, cannot itself satisfy the criteria]**

These omissions limit direct comparability with the eventual official vote percentage.
- There is a source-level inconsistency in the supplied material: one Reuters-based account says **“55% of respondents intended to vote for Hichilema,”** while another says **“55% indicated they planned to vote for Hichilema.”** The numerical figure is the same, but the wording and denominator are not further specified.

- Mwale argued that large opposition rallies should not necessarily be interpreted as evidence of electoral strength, saying the Patriotic Front had held **“big rallies”** before the **2021** election but still lost. [C: Zambia: News Diggers!; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- Mwale was described as campaigning for Hichilema. [C: Zambia: News Diggers!; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- This prediction is **[PRE-WINDOW — occurred before question open, cannot itself satisfy the criteria]**.
- Mwale’s **75 percent** estimate is an individual partisan or aligned political prediction, not an official estimate or a documented poll.

### Amos Chanda assessment

- Former presidential aide **Amos Chanda**, appearing on Prime TV’s *Oxygen of Democracy* programme on **2026-06-25**, said it was **“insane”** to project a **95%** victory for any candidate in the August 13 election. [C: Zambia: News Diggers!; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- Chanda identified Hichilema and Brian Mundubile as the two leading candidates. [C: Zambia: News Diggers!; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- He argued that, in a competitive multiparty democracy, no candidate could realistically secure **70% or 80%** of the vote, and said that anyone claiming a **90%** victory margin was **“actually lying to them.”** [C: Zambia: News Diggers!; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- These comments are **[PRE-WINDOW — occurred before question open, cannot itself satisfy the criteria]**.
- Chanda’s remarks provide a contrasting expert/political judgment against very high estimates such as Mwale’s **75 percent**, though they do not offer a specific central estimate for Hichilema.

## 3. Candidate field

- A Deutsche Welle report dated **2026-06-23** said Hichilema was facing **13 other candidates**, including Brian Mundubile and former lawmaker Given Katuta. [B/C: Deutsche Welle; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- A Vatican News report dated **2026-07-17** said the ECZ had confirmed **12 candidates**, including:
  - Hakainde Hichilema of the United Party for National Development;
  - Brian Mundubile of the National Reconciliation Party for Unity and Prosperity;
  - Harry Kalaba of Citizens First; and
  - Fred M’membe of the Socialist Party. [C: Vatican News, reporting an ECZ confirmation]
- The two accounts conflict: **13 other candidates** would imply **14 total candidates**, whereas **12 candidates** implies **11 other candidates**. The **July 17** account is later and attributes its number to the ECZ, but the supplied research does not include a direct ECZ candidate-list publication to independently verify either figure.
- The number of candidates is relevant because Hichilema himself warned that independent candidates could split the vote.

## 4. Hichilema’s campaign warning about vote splitting

- On **2026-07-02**, Hichilema urged voters to support the UPND and warned that independent candidates would split the ruling party’s vote. [C: Zambia: News Diggers!; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- He said the opposition was using independent candidates to **“take away my votes.”** [C: Zambia: News Diggers!; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- In Solwezi, he told residents to vote for the ruling party if they wanted a university in North Western Province. [C: Zambia: News Diggers!; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]

- These are campaign statements and should be treated as Hichilema’s strategic assessment or appeal, not as independent evidence that vote splitting will occur.

## 5. Economic and political environment

### Economic indicators

- Zambia’s annual inflation reportedly fell to **6.5% in June 2026**, described in one account as the lowest level in over **eight years**. [B: Reuters-based reporting republished by Afrimag/Africanews/MarketScreener; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- The IMF reportedly projected Zambia’s economic growth at **4.3% for 2026**, up from **3.8%** the previous year. [B: Reuters-based reporting republished by Afrimag/Africanews/MarketScreener; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- Reports attribute the economic improvement to debt restructuring, higher copper prices, and fiscal measures. [B/C: Reuters-based reports and Deutsche Welle; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- Zambia was described as Africa’s or the world’s second-largest copper producer, depending on the account. [B/C: Reuters-based reports and Deutsche Welle; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- Despite the macroeconomic improvements, reports say the cost of living remains high and many households remain in poverty or are struggling with hunger. [B/C: Reuters-based reports and Deutsche Welle; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- Brian Mundubile cited government foreign reserves of **$6.5 billion** while arguing that citizens were still suffering. His reported quote was: **“How can you boast that you have built $6.5 billion in foreign reserves when your people are going hungry?”** [B: Reuters-based reporting republished by Afrimag/MarketScreener; **SINGLE-SOURCE as to the quote and figure**]
- The supplied research does not provide an official source for the **$6.5 billion** reserve figure or specify the measurement date, composition, or whether it refers to gross or net reserves.

### Opposition criticisms

- Opposition figures accuse the Hichilema government of obstructing opposition campaigning and repressing dissent. [B/C: Reuters-based reports; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- Critics cite a **2025** cybercrime or cyber-security law, which civil-society groups claim threatens freedom of expression or silences critics. [B/C: Reuters-based reporting; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- Mundubile told Reuters: **“Any dissident voice is considered an enemy of the State.”** [B: Reuters]
- Reports also mention constitutional changes expanding parliament as a source of criticism. [C: MarketScreener, reporting the broader election story; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- The President denies the accusations of repression. [B/C: Reuters-based reports; **SINGLE-SOURCE**
- Former Justice Minister **Princess Kasune**, speaking on **2026-07-17**, defended the Cyber Security Act and said the opposition was misinforming Zambians. [C: Zambia: News Diggers!; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- Kasune said the law is designed to protect innocent people and distinguish between **“friendly”** and **“obedient”** users and those who violate laws or other people’s rights. [C: Zambia: News Diggers!; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- Kasune also predicted that Hichilema would receive **six million votes** in the August election. [C: Zambia: News Diggers!; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- The supplied material does not provide the expected total number of valid presidential votes, so the **six million votes** prediction cannot be converted into a percentage from the available information.
- Kasune’s forecast is post-window and therefore potentially relevant to the forecasting period, but it is a statement by a former government minister defending Hichilema, not an independent poll or official projection.

### Voter concerns

- A Deutsche Welle report dated **2026-06-23** identified unemployment and the cost of living as leading voter concerns. [B/C: Deutsche Welle; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]
- The same report said the election could affect Zambia’s role in the global critical-minerals market and its relations with China and the United States. [B/C: Deutsche Welle; **SINGLE-SOURCE**]

# Source-quality and contradiction assessment

1. **No official ECZ results are present.** All vote-share figures in the research are either a pre-election survey or political predictions.
2. The **55%** survey figure is the strongest quantitative electoral datapoint supplied, because it is attributed to the Zambia Election Research Network and reported by Reuters. However, it was conducted at the end of **2025**, before the forecasting window, and its methodology is absent.
3. The **75 percent** and **six million votes** figures are political predictions from individuals publicly aligned with or defending Hichilema. They should not be treated as independent forecasts.
4. The **75 percent** prediction conflicts qualitatively with Amos Chanda’s view that **70% or 80%** victories are unrealistic, but Chanda did not issue a precise Hichilema estimate.
5. Candidate totals conflict:
   - Deutsche Welle: **13 other candidates**;
   - Vatican News, citing ECZ: **12 candidates**.
   The later ECZ-attributed figure is more directly relevant, but the supplied research lacks the primary ECZ candidate list.
6. The research does not provide:
   - Hichilema’s 2021 vote percentage or vote count;
   - current polling averages;
   - sample sizes or methods for the Zambia Election Research Network survey;
   - turnout projections;
   - regional or demographic breakdowns;
   - verified campaign-event attendance;
   - official information on valid votes or the expected denominator needed to translate **six million votes** into a percentage;
   - any official ECZ update on election readiness or the candidate list beyond the report quoted by Vatican News.

Overall, the supplied evidence establishes Hichilema as the incumbent and a reported favorite, with one pre-window survey at **55%**, one pre-window partisan prediction at **75 percent**, and one post-window partisan prediction of **six million votes**. None of these figures is an official first-round result.
