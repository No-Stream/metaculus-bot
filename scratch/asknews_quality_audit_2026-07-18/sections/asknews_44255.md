## News Articles (AskNews)
#### Intelligence Briefing: H.R.6644, the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act  
**Forecast question:** Will H.R.6644 become law before **July 4, 2026**?

### Bottom line
Based on the research provided, the most likely outcome is **Yes**: H.R.6644 has already passed both chambers, has been sent to President Trump, and multiple sources report that he is **refusing to sign it** but will allow the **10-day window excluding Sundays** to expire, which would make it law without his signature.  
That said, as of the latest reports in the research, the bill appears to be **pending enactment**, not yet formally signed.

#### 2) Bill content and policy details relevant to passage dynamics
### Institutional investor restrictions
- The final bill retains a prohibition barring **“large institutional investors”** from purchasing single-family homes.  
- A “large institutional investor” is defined as a **for-profit entity controlling 350 or more single-family homes**.
  - **Source:** JD Supra (**June 30, 2026 08:52 PM**), National Law Review (**June 26, 2026 12:46 PM**).
  - **Credibility:** Moderately high; legal analysis sources provide specific definitions, but the final statutory text should be verified against Congress.gov for ultimate precision.

### Timing of provisions
- The prohibition is described as taking effect **180 days after enactment** and being **repealed after 15 years**.
  - **Source:** JD Supra (**June 30, 2026 08:52 PM**), National Law Review (**June 26, 2026 12:46 PM**).
  - **Credibility:** Moderate to high; this is a legal summary of the bill text.

### Penalties and compliance
- Civil penalties can reach **$1 million per violation** or **three times the purchase price**.
  - **Source:** JD Supra (**June 30, 2026 08:52 PM**), National Law Review (**June 26, 2026 12:46 PM**).
  - **Credibility:** Moderate to high.

- Large institutional investors must report holdings to HUD on a **city-by-city basis within 180 days of enactment and annually thereafter**.
  - **Source:** National Law Review (**June 26, 2026 12:46 PM**).
  - **Credibility:** Moderate to high.

### Build-to-Rent and exception structure
- JD Supra says the final bill:
  - eliminates the Senate’s **7-year forced disposal requirement** for Build-to-Rent (BTR) communities,
  - removes renter “first-look” and right-of-first-refusal (ROFR) provisions from the BTR exception,
  - allows BTR projects acquired as “excepted purchases” under **Section 1001(a)(2)(B)** to be held **indefinitely**.
  - **Source:** JD Supra (**June 30, 2026 08:52 PM**).
  - **Credibility:** Moderate; specific and detailed, but from a legal newsletter rather than primary text.

- The National Law Review says exceptions include:
  - Build-to-Rent (BTR) programs,
  - newly built or renovated homes intended for sale,
  - renovate-to-rent programs requiring at least **15%** of the purchase price invested in structural/core improvements,
  - homeownership initiatives,
  - senior housing (**55+**),
  - sales between institutional investors.
  - **Source:** National Law Review (**June 26, 2026 12:46 PM**).
  - **Credibility:** Moderate to high.

### Other policy items mentioned
- The bill reportedly:
  - streamlines environmental reviews for small projects of **15 units or fewer**,
  - increases FHA multifamily mortgage limits,
  - creates a **$200 million annual Innovation Fund**,
  - includes a HUD-mandated toll-free resource for renters,
  - includes annual reporting by large investors.
  - **Source:** JD Supra (**June 30, 2026 08:52 PM**).
  - **Credibility:** Moderate; detailed but not primary-source reporting.

- Another source says the bill expands financing, increases the maximum eligible income for HUD’s HOME Investment Partnership Program, and establishes a grant program for state and local affordable housing strategies.
  - **Source:** Ripon Advance (**June 24, 2026 05:26 PM**).
  - **Credibility:** Moderate.

- E&E News says the bill eases permitting requirements and reauthorizes a disaster recovery program.
  - **Source:** E&E News (**June 23, 2026 10:21 AM**).
  - **Credibility:** High relative to the rest because E&E News is a specialized policy outlet.

### Miscellaneous legislative provisions mentioned in some sources
- BeInCrypto says the bill includes a provision banning the Federal Reserve from issuing a central bank digital currency (CBDC) through **December 31, 2030**.
  - **Source:** BeInCrypto (**June 23, 2026 11:30 AM**).
  - **Credibility:** Lower to moderate; this is a crypto-focused outlet and the provision is unusual enough that it should be verified against the actual bill text.
  - This same source states:
    - Title XI, Section 1101 prohibits the Fed from issuing or creating a CBDC or any substantially similar digital asset,
    - stablecoins are carved out as “open, permissionless, and private dollar assets.”
  - These claims are not repeated elsewhere in the provided research.

- No source reports a repeal, court injunction, or procedural obstacle that would prevent the bill from becoming law before the July 4 deadline.

### Contradictions and source conflicts

#### 1) Whether Trump supported the bill
- **Earlier sources**:  
  - E&E News (**June 23, 2026**) says the compromise measure has buy-in from the Trump administration and is expected to pass the House easily, with Trump potentially signing it this week.
  - Architectural Digest (**June 24, 2026**) says it is expected to become law with Trump’s signature on **June 24, 2026**.
  - National Catholic Register (**June 24, 2026**) says it is expected to be signed into law by Trump on **June 24, 2026**.
- **Later sources**:
  - By **June 29-30, 2026**, multiple outlets report Trump canceled the signing, called the bill a **“yawn”** / **“a big yawn,”** and intends to let it become law without signing, or is using the bill as leverage for the SAVE America Act.

### Assessment
This is not necessarily a true factual contradiction about the bill itself; it appears to reflect a **change in Trump’s position over time**. Earlier optimism about a quick signature was superseded by later reporting of refusal/delay.

#### 2) “Expected to become law without signature” vs “expected to be signed”
- The research contains both:
  - expectations of a direct signature on **June 24, 2026**, and
  - later expectations that it will become law **without** signature after the 10-day period.
- This is a time-based change, not a permanent inconsistency. The later reporting is more relevant to the current forecast.

#### 3) CBDC provision
- Only **BeInCrypto** mentions the CBDC ban through **December 31, 2030**.
- No other source in the provided research mentions this.
- This could indicate either an omitted but real provision or a report-specific emphasis. It should be treated cautiously until cross-checked with Congress.gov.
