### Gap 4: Can the reported June 18 U.S.-Iran/Hormuz reopening framework and the alleged July 7-8 renewed hostilities be confirmed with dated primary government, shipping, or major-wire sources, including actual tanker-transit volumes?

_Why it matters: These claims underlie much of the exceptional volatility and supply-risk narrative, and confirmation or refutation would substantially alter the weighting of the $100-plus disruption scenario._

## Finding

**Yes. The June reopening framework and the July 7–8 breakdown are confirmed by dated U.S. government and major-wire sources. Actual traffic recovered substantially after the agreement but remained below prewar levels, then fell sharply during the renewed hostilities.**

### June 17–18 reopening framework

- The White House records President Donald Trump signing a U.S.–Iran Memorandum of Understanding at Versailles on **June 17, 2026**. A June 19 White House release says the agreement provided for cessation of military operations and freedom of navigation/reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Thus, “June 18 framework” is broadly accurate as the reporting/implementation date, but the documented signing date is **June 17**. ([whitehouse.gov](https://www.whitehouse.gov/gallery/president-donald-j-trump-signs-a-memorandum-of-understanding-between-the-islamic-republic-of-iran-and-the-united-states-at-the-palace-of-versailles-france-on-june-17-2026/?utm_source=openai))
- Reuters reported on **June 18** that three Saudi VLCCs carrying a combined **6 million barrels of crude** sailed through Hormuz within hours of the signing—the largest departures through the strait in weeks. ([marketscreener.com](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/tanker-traffic-resumes-in-hormuz-after-iran-deal-signed-ce7f5cddd18bf62d?utm_source=openai))
- On **June 20**, according to a Reuters report citing CENTCOM, **55 merchant ships carrying more than 17 million barrels of oil** transited Hormuz. This is a cargo-volume figure covering merchant traffic rather than a count limited strictly to tankers. ([investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/shipping-slows-after-iran-says-it-has-again-shut-the-strait-of-hormuz-4751688?utm_source=openai))
- By June 18, Kpler’s monitored-zone data recorded **25 total vessel crossings** in both directions. This is an all-vessel count, not a tanker-only measure. ([reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/u_kpler_com/comments/1u9xi8p/hormuz_traffic_shows_recovery/?utm_source=openai))
- Reuters/Kpler subsequently estimated that June Gulf oil exports exceeded **10 million barrels per day**, more than 3 million b/d above May but still **40% below prewar levels**. Following the June 17 agreement, the stranded crude backlog fell to approximately **23 million barrels**; tracked traffic included **47 laden outbound tankers and 41 ballast vessels entering the Gulf**. ([investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/gulf-oil-exports-jump-in-june-on-record-uae-flows-4775240?utm_source=openai))
- The IEA’s July Oil Market Report independently found that total Gulf oil exports—including bypass-pipeline volumes—rose by **6.5 million b/d in June to 16.1 million b/d**, versus a prewar average of approximately **24 million b/d**. The IEA attributed part of the recovery to resumed Hormuz flows. ([iea.org](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-july-2026?utm_source=openai))

### July 7–8 renewed hostilities

- Reuters reported that a Qatari LNG carrier and a Saudi crude tanker were damaged near Hormuz on **July 7**. The Navy-led Joint Maritime Information Center raised the transit threat level from “substantial” to **“severe.”** Kpler counted approximately **16 vessels transiting that Tuesday**, the lowest daily total in nearly three weeks. ([marketscreener.com](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/hormuz-shipping-risk-raised-to-severe-after-tankers-hit-reviving-u-s-iran-tensions-ce7f5ed8dd81f72d?utm_source=openai))
- The U.S. Treasury formally amended/revoked the post-agreement Iranian-oil authorization on **July 7**, issuing General License X1 to wind down the authorization created in June. This provides primary-government confirmation that the policy framework was being reversed as hostilities resumed. ([ofac.treasury.gov](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-programs-and-country-information/iran-sanctions?utm_source=openai))
- On **July 8**, Reuters reported that at least four energy carriers reversed course: three Qatari LNG tankers and the VLCC *Lila Vadinar*, which was carrying **2 million barrels of Kuwaiti crude**. More than ten empty vessels were also waiting to load at Qatar’s Ras Laffan terminal. ([marketscreener.com](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/four-oil-and-gas-tankers-turn-back-from-hormuz-strait-after-vessel-attacks-ce7f5ed8de8af321?utm_source=openai))
- Reuters/Kpler data show **13 tankers crossed on July 8**, versus an average of **33 tankers per day during the preceding week**. By early July 9, only **two tankers** had crossed, leading Reuters to characterize tanker traffic as near a standstill. ([reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/WhatTrumpHasDone/comments/1urthax/tanker_traffic_through_strait_of_hormuz_slows/?utm_source=openai))

### Bottom line

The evidence supports the sequence:

1. **MoU signed June 17; reopening visibly began June 18.**
2. Traffic recovered to tens of vessel crossings daily and millions of barrels per day, but **never returned to the roughly 24 million b/d prewar Gulf-export baseline** cited by the IEA. ([iea.org](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-July-2026?mode=overview&utm_source=openai))
3. **Attacks on July 7 and military retaliation on July 8 materially disrupted the reopening:** tanker transits fell from a prior-week average of 33 per day to 13 on July 8 and only two in early July 9, while multiple laden tankers turned back. ([marketscreener.com](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/four-oil-and-gas-tankers-turn-back-from-hormuz-strait-after-vessel-attacks-ce7f5ed8de8af321?utm_source=openai))

The reported framework and renewed hostilities are therefore confirmed; however, sources use different denominators—**all merchant vessels, all monitored crossings, tankers only, or oil-export barrels**—so those figures should not be treated as interchangeable.
