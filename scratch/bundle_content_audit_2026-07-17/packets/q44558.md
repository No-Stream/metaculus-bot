### What counts as a Level 4 advisory

- The State Department defines **Level 4 — “Do Not Travel”** as its highest advisory level, used when travelers face a greater likelihood of life-threatening risks and the U.S. government may have very limited or no ability to assist them. [U.S. State Department — Travel Advisories](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories.html/) ([travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories..html?utm_source=openai))
- The department says Level 3 and Level 4 advisories are reviewed **at least every six months**, but they may also be updated whenever conditions or U.S. government staffing restrictions change substantially. Thus, an advisory published in August need not represent a newly dangerous country; it may be a routine reissuance or reaffirmation of an existing Level 4 designation. [U.S. State Department — Travel Advisories](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories.html/) ([travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories.html/?_sfd=4ulwik&utm_source=openai))
- The State Department’s internal guidance says levels can change in advance of, or in response to, natural disasters, disease outbreaks, political events, changes in embassy operations, and other security developments. Assessments use reporting from diplomatic posts, other U.S. agencies, foreign governments, and credible open sources. [Foreign Affairs Manual, 7 FAM Appendix A](https://fam.state.gov/FAM/07FAM/07FAM0000apA.html) ([fam.state.gov](https://fam.state.gov/FAM/07FAM/07FAM0000apA.html?utm_source=openai))

### Scheduling implications

Because every Level 3/4 advisory must ordinarily be reviewed within six months, the approximately two dozen existing Level 4 countries create a recurring flow of reaffirmations. However:
