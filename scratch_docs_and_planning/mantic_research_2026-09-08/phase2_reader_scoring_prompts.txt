Mantic's Crucible tournament ("Preseason 2", the Series 2 trial) has switched its leaderboard from spot PEER to spot BASELINE scoring, which I confirmed from the live API (`score_type: "spot_baseline_tournament"` on https://competitions.mantic.com/api/projects/tournaments/preseason-2/, versus `spot_peer_tournament` on series-1 and practice-series-1). Baseline scoring is strictly proper, so it does NOT change the optimal forecast; what changes is where the points live. Three things follow, and they are not the three the brief anticipated.

First, loosening our clamps is NOT worth it. The binary clamp at 0.02/0.98 has a hard ceiling of +2.73 baseline points per question even against a genuinely certain outcome, and it only pays to loosen if our >=0.98 calls are wrong less than 1 time in 157 (exact proper-score crossover, derived below). Series 1 ran 489 questions and ZERO of them were binary, because Mantic's own "discrimination hierarchy" rule actively rewrites binary questions into numeric or date form. The MC floor of 0.01 is not even ours to relax (forecasting-tools 0.2.92 hard-codes it in the `PredictedOptionList` validator), it binds on 41% of real Mantic MC option-probabilities because Mantic MC questions have up to 50 options, and I measured its expected cost at only 0.70 baseline points per ballot (max 6.64 on a 50-option question). MC is 5% of the question pool. Total expected value of all clamp loosening across a 500-question series: roughly 40 to 80 points out of a plausible 25,000. Refuse it.

Second, the real money is in the OUT-OF-BOUND bucket on quantitative questions, and specifically on date questions. 28% of Series 1's 520 resolved questions resolved outside the question's own range, and 53.7% of the 188 date questions with an open upper bound resolved ABOVE it. The platform gives that bucket a fixed 0.05 reference while an inbound bin gets about 0.0045, so the bucket where half of all date questions land is the single highest-variance number in the whole forecast: on real Series 1 questions the cross-bot score spread on one out-of-bound date question reached 338 points (-195 to +143). I scraped all ten competing bots' full submitted CDFs off five resolved posts per question type (35 posts, 274 continuous forecast-rows, via the undocumented `disagreement_forecasts` object in the public page payload) and scored every one of them under the real backend formula. The bot with the most generous open-tail mass was the best scorer on the raw sample, and the four worst individual scores in the whole sample were bots sitting at the platform's 0.001 tail floor when the question resolved there.

Third, Mantic Series 2 added two question shapes that are live in Preseason 2 right now and that our pipeline has no representation for. Post 650 is a MULTI-RESOLUTION question ("What will the price of bitcoin be on each day from 8 to 18 September 2026?", `multi_resolution: true`, 11 daily resolutions scored against one submitted distribution and averaged). I derived the optimum (it is the expected empirical distribution of the resolution set, a mixture, not a predictive distribution for any single instance) and priced the error: submitting a single-day distribution costs 38.7 baseline points on that question. Post 651 is a 12-bin day-granularity DATE question in which 3 of the 12 bins are calendar-impossible by the question's own fine print (Sat 12, Sun 13 and Sat 19 September are not trading sessions), which our percentile-to-PCHIP path structurally cannot zero out; that smearing costs 14.4 baseline points on that question even with zero information.
-- FACTS --
SCORING TYPE. https://competitions.mantic.com/api/projects/tournaments/preseason-2/ returns `score_type: "spot_baseline_tournament"`; series-1 and practice-series-1 both return `spot_peer_tournament`. In the open-source Metaculus backend, scoring/constants.py `LeaderboardScoreTypes.get_base_score` maps `SPOT_BASELINE_TOURNAMENT` to `ScoreTypes.SPOT_BASELINE`, which dispatches to `evaluate_forecasts_baseline_spot_forecast` (scoring/score_math.py:111). Do NOT read the per-question `default_score_type`: on Preseason-2 post 650 it still says `"peer"`, a leftover; the project-level `score_type` is what drives the leaderboard.
BASELINE FORMULA, categorical (binary and multiple choice), from scoring/score_math.py:97 and :138: `100 * np.log(p * options_at_time) / np.log(options_at_time)`, i.e. 100*(log_N(p) + 1). This matches the Series 2 doc §4 exactly, so binary and MC scoring on Mantic is IDENTICAL to Metaculus baseline. Binary values: p=0.5 -> 0.00, p=0.98 -> +97.09, p=0.99 -> +98.55, p=0.999 -> +99.86; and if wrong, p=0.98 -> -464.39, p=0.99 -> -564.39, p=0.999 -> -896.58.
BASELINE FORMULA, continuous (numeric, discrete, date), from scoring/score_math.py:100-103 and :143-145: `baseline = 0.05` if the resolution bucket is index 0 or the last index, else `baseline = (1 - 0.05*open_bounds_count) / (len(pmf) - 2)`; then `forecast_score = 100 * np.log(pmf[resolution_bucket] / baseline) / 2`. The pmf has length inbound_outcome_count + 2 (index 0 is mass below the lower bound, index -1 is mass above the upper bound). So the coefficient is 50 points per nat, and for a doubly-open 200-bin question the inbound reference is 0.9/200 = 0.0045 while the out-of-bound reference is 0.05 (11.1x more generous).
BASELINE SCORING IS STRICTLY PROPER, including the bucket-dependent 0.05 reference. Score(bucket k) = 50*ln(p_k) + c_k where c_k = -50*ln(baseline_k). Expected score under true belief q is 50*sum_k q_k*ln(p_k) + sum_k q_k*c_k; the second term does not depend on p, so the argmax is p = q. A bucket-dependent ADDITIVE offset cannot break propriety. Therefore switching from peer to baseline does not change the optimal distribution width, the optimal tail mass, or anything else about what we should submit.
CLAMPS ARE THE PLATFORM'S, AND THEY ARE 0.001/0.999. From the Metaculus backend questions/serializers/common.py: binary `probability_yes` must satisfy 0.001 <= p <= 0.999 (line 464); every current MC option must satisfy 0.001 <= v <= 0.999 (line 496); for an open lower bound `continuous_cdf[0] >= 0.001` (line 556) and for an open upper bound `continuous_cdf[-1] <= 0.999` (line 570); for a CLOSED lower bound cdf[0] must equal exactly 0.0 and for a closed upper bound cdf[-1] must equal exactly 1.0. Per-bin: min step `round(0.01/inbound_outcome_count, 9)` checked against a 9-decimal-rounded pmf, max step the UNROUNDED `0.2 * 200 / inbound_outcome_count`. This is exactly what metaculus_bot/numeric/config.py `grid_step_constraints` already implements, including the 450-bin rounding trap, which is the live Preseason-2 bin count on post 650.
COST OF OUR BINARY CLAMP, expected-score argument. Reporting 0.98 instead of a looser value at true probability q: at q=0.98 loosening to 0.999 costs -5.93 points, at q=0.99 it costs -1.58, at q=0.9936 it is exactly break-even, at q=0.999 it gains +2.34, and the asymptotic ceiling as q approaches 1 is +2.73. The exact proper-score crossover for moving a clamp from a to b is q* = ln((1-a)/(1-b)) / [ln(b/a) + ln((1-a)/(1-b))]: 0.98->0.99 gives q*=0.98556 (wrong less than 1 in 69), 0.98->0.995 gives 0.98916 (1 in 92), 0.98->0.999 gives 0.99363 (1 in 157), 0.99->0.999 gives 0.99609 (1 in 255). This is a purely empirical calibration test on our own archive, and the repo already has the tool (metaculus_bot/performance_analysis/clip_threshold.py), though it currently prices candidates in spot-peer terms via `spot_peer_delta` and would need a baseline objective added.
BINARY IS ALMOST ABSENT ON MANTIC. Across all 489 series-1 questions plus 32 practice questions the type mix was 200 date, 157 discrete, 137 numeric, 26 multiple_choice and ZERO binary. Preseason-2 has 1 binary of 4. The tournament rules doc §(3) Discrimination states an explicit hierarchy ('Numerical or date' > 'Multiple choice' > 'Binary') and says an automated system detects and rewrites binary questions into more discriminating forms. So 94% of the Mantic question pool is quantitative and the binary clamp is a near-empty surface.
OUR BINARY CLAMP IS OURS TO CHANGE; OUR MC FLOOR IS NOT. constants.py:467-468 sets BINARY_PROB_MIN/MAX = 0.02/0.98, applied at forecaster_runners.py:161 and stacking.py:113; forecasting-tools 0.2.92 only clamps binary to [0.001, 0.999] (binary_report.py:26-37), so 0.02/0.98 is entirely our choice. By contrast forecasting_tools/data_models/multiple_choice_report.py:42-43 hard-codes `max_clamped_prob = 0.99` and `min_clamped_prob = 0.01` in the `PredictedOptionList` model validator and raises ValueError when the clamp-and-renormalize moves any option by more than 0.05 (line 64). constants.py:530-531 documents MC_PROB_MIN/MAX = 0.01/0.99 as deliberately ALIGNED to that validator, and mc_processing.py's docstring already states the key fact: 'ft clamps to its OWN hard-coded 0.01 regardless of what we send, so a lower floor here changes nothing'.
MEASURED COST OF THE 0.01 MC FLOOR ON REAL MANTIC BALLOTS. Across 821 option-probabilities in 43 real bot ballots on 5 resolved Mantic MC questions, 333 (40.6%) sit at or below 0.01 and 37 sit at the platform floor 0.001, because Mantic MC questions carry up to 50 options (observed K values: 50, 22, 14, 13, 6). Replaying each ballot through our clamp and pricing it as -(100/ln K) * KL(ballot || clamped ballot): mean cost 0.70 baseline points, max 6.64 (the 50-option question). Two of the seven 50-option ballots would have moved by more than 0.05 (0.051 and 0.093) and so would trip ft's ValidationError if fed to it unclamped, but our pipeline clamps BEFORE constructing PredictedOptionList (mc_processing.py `build_mc_prediction`), so ft's validator is a no-op and we do not hit that raise. The mc_processing.py docstring's claim that 'Metaculus MC ballots are far below this cardinality in practice' is true for Metaculus and false for Mantic.

Of 520 resolved series-1 questions (31 annulled or ambiguous excluded), 146 resolved outside the range: 136 above the upper bound and 10 below the lower. By type: date 102/192 (53.1%), discrete 35/141 (24.8%), numeric 9/133 (6.8%), multiple_choice 0/26. Restricting to date questions that actually had an OPEN upper bound: 101 of 188 resolved above it, 53.7%. Bound structure of series-1 quantitative questions: 285 closed-lower/open-upper, 179 fully open, 27 fully closed, 3 open-lower/closed-upper.
SCORE IN THE OUT-OF-BOUND BUCKET, with its 0.05 reference and the 50-points-per-nat coefficient: p=0.001 (the platform floor) scores -195.6, p=0.01 scores -80.5, p=0.05 scores exactly 0.0, p=0.10 scores +34.7, p=0.20 scores +69.3, p=0.35 scores +97.3, p=0.50 scores +115.1, p=0.90 scores +144.5. The reachable maximum in an inbound bin is +189.7 for a doubly-open question, and it is INVARIANT to bin count (200, 1000 and 2000 bins all give +189.7) because the server's max step 0.2*200/inbound and the reference (1-0.05*obc)/inbound scale together. So our PCHIP-on-the-question-grid approach is already bin-count-neutral and needs no change on that account.
MEASURED BOT BEHAVIOUR (scraped from the public `disagreement_forecasts` object embedded in the Next.js flight payload of https://competitions.mantic.com/questions/<id>/ for 35 resolved series-1 posts; it exposes every competing bot's full CDF and pmf plus a Mantic-specific `continuous_pmf_floor: 0.001`, and it is NOT in the published OpenAPI spec). In-sample on 10 date questions, so read the +14 point headline as suggestive; the optimum sitting at the true base rate is what propriety predicts and is a sanity check, not an independent finding.
MULTI-RESOLUTION OPTIMUM, derived. Series 2 doc §7 gives the score as sum_i (c_i / C) * [100*(log_N(p_i)+1)] with c_i the count of resolutions in bucket i and C their total, i.e. the resolution-weighted average of the per-resolution baseline scores. Since that is linear in c given p, the expected score is (k/C) * sum_i E[c_i] * ln(p_i) + const, maximised at p_i = E[c_i]/C. So the optimal submission is the expected EMPIRICAL distribution of the resolution set: the equally weighted mixture of the per-instance predictive distributions, not the predictive distribution of any one instance. The cost of submitting q instead is exactly (points-per-nat) * KL(mixture || q).
MULTI-RESOLUTION PRICED ON THE LIVE QUESTION. Preseason-2 post 650, 'What will the price of bitcoin be on each day from 8 to 18 September 2026?', has `multi_resolution: true`, `inbound_outcome_count: 450`, `precision: 100.0`, range [54950, 99950], both bounds open, and its resolution criteria state eleven daily values each scored and then averaged. At a 77,000 spot and 2.5% daily log-vol, and applying the server's real per-bin floor 0.0000222 and cap 0.088888888: submitting the day-one distribution scores +8.2 against the mixture's +46.9, a loss of 38.7 baseline points; the mid-window single-day distribution loses 0.5; the terminal-day distribution loses 4.3. Add a 1%/day drift and the terminal-value forecast loses 12.2 and a no-drift mixture loses 21.8. The correct mixture width is sigma * sqrt(mean t) = 6.1%, between the day-one 2.5% and the day-eleven 8.3%. Under Mantic's proposed unified continuous coefficient these losses shrink by a factor of 100/ln(450)/50 = 0.327, so 38.7 becomes 12.7 and the ranking is unchanged.
THE MULTI-RESOLUTION COUNT IS NOT IN THE API. Post 650 exposes `multi_resolution: true` but `resolutions: null` while open (it presumably fills at resolution). The number of resolution instances appears only in the resolution-criteria prose ('There are therefore eleven daily resolution values...'). So a prompt clause must not interpolate N; the forecaster has to read the count out of the criteria itself. `multi_resolution` and `resolutions` are live API fields but are absent from the published OpenAPI spec at /tmp/mantic_probe/ref/mantic_openapi.yml.
SERIES 2 COARSE-GRID DATE QUESTIONS CONTAIN IMPOSSIBLE BINS. Preseason-2 post 651, 'On which date will the S&P 500 post its largest single-day percentage move between 8 and 18 September 2026?', is a date question with `date_granularity: "day"`, `inbound_outcome_count: 12`, BOTH bounds closed, and range [1788825600, 1789862400] which is exactly 12 one-day bins covering 8 to 19 September. Its fine print says only the nine listed trading sessions are eligible, so bins 5 (Sat 12 Sep), 6 (Sun 13 Sep) and 12 (Sat 19 Sep) are impossible by construction. A forecast that smears uniformly over 12 bins instead of 9 loses 50 * ln((1/9)/(1/12)) = 14.4 baseline points with zero information, and more once the model has a real view. Our percentile-to-PCHIP representation cannot express a zero interior bin. Separately, `grid_step_constraints(13)` returns max_step = 1.0 for this grid, so the server imposes no concentration cap at all here; the limit is entirely our interpolation.
MANTIC'S OWN PLANNED CHANGE TO THE CONTINUOUS COEFFICIENT IS AMBIGUOUS BUT HARMLESS. Series 2 doc §4 says 'For numerical questions the score is similar but with a scaling factor that was set empirically... We are going to use the same, unadjusted score for all question types.' Read literally that replaces the /2 in score_math.py:103 with /ln(N) where N is the bin count, dropping the coefficient from 50 to 100/ln(200) = 18.88 points per nat at 200 bins and 16.36 at 450 bins, which compresses continuous scores about 2.65x relative to binary and MC. The formula images in the doc did not survive text extraction, so I cannot confirm whether the 0.05 out-of-bound reservation survives. Because both candidate formulas are strictly proper and differ only by a positive multiplicative constant and an additive per-bucket constant, the ambiguity does not change a single number we should submit. It only changes which question types dominate the leaderboard: under the current formula a continuous question is worth up to +190 and a binary up to +100; under the unified rule a 200-bin continuous question caps at +69.6.
MISSING A QUESTION IS EXPENSIVE UNDER THE NEW RULES, AND I MEASURED IT. The Series 2 doc §4 sets three safety nets: the leaderboard is the sum of your top 95% of forecasts by baseline points, a MISSED question receives the 25th-percentile score rather than 0, and a question with fewer than two forecasts is excluded. On my 30-question continuous sample the mean 25th-percentile score was +21.4 while the mean median score was +48.5, so a median-quality answer beats the fallback by 27.1 points per question (date 26.3, discrete 20.9, numeric 34.1). Under the old peer scoring the equivalent gap was near zero, which is exactly the incentive change Mantic set out to make. Top-95% trimming discards ceil(0.05N) of your worst scores (5 at N=100, 25 at N=500); on my sample dropping each bot's worst 5% raised mean score by +8.6 to +16.7 points, so a rare catastrophic score is partly subsidised, but every competitor gets the same subsidy so it is not an edge.
OPERATIONAL CONSTRAINTS THAT BEAR ON SCORING. The rules doc states that up to 3 questions are released per hour on the hour and are open for ONE hour, with a one-time forecast per question; combined with the 27-point cost of a miss, wall-clock robustness inside a 60-minute window is worth more than any clamp change. Under spot baseline the score reads whichever forecast is active at `spot_scoring_time` (score_math.py:120-124 requires `start <= spot_forecast_timestamp < end`); Mantic forecasts carry `end_time: null` so ours stays active, but a WITHDRAWN forecast sets end_time and would score 0 rather than the 25th-percentile fallback. Post 650's `spot_scoring_time` is 2026-09-20T12:00:00Z, the tournament close, not the question's hour.
PLATFORM IDENTITY IS ALREADY READABLE AT PROMPT-BUILD TIME WITH NO PLUMBING. `question.page_url` is set for both platforms: forecasting_tools/data_models/questions.py:179 sets `https://www.metaculus.com/questions/{post_id}` and metaculus_bot/mantic.py:79 overwrites it with `https://competitions.mantic.com/questions/{id_of_post}/`. The constants already exist (constants.py:64 MANTIC_HOST, :78 METACULUS_HOST, :79 QUESTION_PLATFORM_HOSTS) and so does the string vocabulary (research/persistence.py:14-15 PLATFORM_METACULUS / PLATFORM_MANTIC). The prompt builders take only (question, research) today (forecaster_runners.py:135, :185, :245), so a leaf helper reading the hostname off page_url needs no signature change and works in stacking.py too. One caveat: page_url is typed `str | None` (questions.py:77) and defaults to None on a locally constructed question, so the helper needs a defined answer for None.
THE PROMPT SCORING WORDING IS THINNER THAN THE BRIEF SUGGESTED. 'Metaculus peer score' appears exactly twice in the repo, at prompts.py:841 (binary_prompt) and prompts.py:1281 (stacking_binary_prompt). The numeric prompt has its own '── Scoring Rule ──' block at prompts.py:1156 describing a continuous log-density score. The MC and stacking-MC/numeric prompts carry no scoring sentence at all. Two accuracy notes on the existing text: (a) the Metaculus tournaments this bot enters are scored SPOT peer, not peer (performance_analysis/platform_scores.py:4-5 documents `score_type=spot_peer_tournament` and `default_score_type=spot_peer`), so the current wording is imprecise for Metaculus too; (b) the numeric block's density framing is actually sound and bin-count-invariant, because the effective density cap is max_step * inbound = 0.2*200 = 40 and the floor is min_step * inbound = 0.01 for any grid of 40 bins or more, which is what the block says. Only its bounds sentence ('Probability mass below/above the bounds is scored as a binary event') is genuinely uninformative under baseline scoring, where that bucket has an explicit 0.05 reference.
THE OPEN-BOUND PROMPT MESSAGING IS ALREADY STRONG. numeric/utils.py:266-292 `bound_messages` already tells the forecaster that an open bound is not a hard limit, that percentiles are the only way to express mass beyond it, that a ~75% belief in exceeding the ceiling requires the P50 to sit above it, and 'Do not pile percentiles at the boundary.' numeric/config.py:128 also carries an OPEN_BOUND_PILING_THRESHOLD of 0.10 diagnostic. So the out-of-bound recommendation below is about supplying the Mantic BASE RATE and about extending this messaging to DateQuestion when Phase 2 lands, not about inventing new instruction.
DATE QUESTIONS ARE CURRENTLY DROPPED at forecaster.py:964 (`elif isinstance(question, (DateQuestion, ConditionalQuestion))`). Since date questions were 200 of 489 series-1 questions (41%) and 192 of the 520 resolved ones, and since they carry both the highest out-of-bound rate (53.7% above an open upper bound) and the widest cross-bot score spreads (mean 151 points), date support is worth more than every clamp and prompt change in this report combined.
-- RECOMMENDATIONS --
RANK 1 (highest impact, moderate cost): Ship DateQuestion support and give it the full `bound_messages` treatment plus the Mantic out-of-bound base rate. Date questions are 41% of the Mantic pool, 53.7% of them with an open upper bound resolve ABOVE it, and their cross-bot score spread averages 151 baseline points, the widest of any type. On my in-sample pricing, going from an empty tail (the 0.001 floor) to a base-rate-informed tail is worth about +14 baseline points per date question, and the loss avoided in the worst case is 195.6 points per question. The specific ask beyond plumbing: make sure `numeric/utils.py:bound_messages` (or its date equivalent) fires for DateQuestion, and add ONE base-rate sentence to the open-upper-bound message when the platform is Mantic, in the house style with the receipt attached. Recommended text: `• On this platform about half of past date questions resolved AFTER the displayed upper bound, so treat 'the event has not happened by {upper}' as a live central case and not a tail: if that is your view, your P50 belongs above {upper} (101 of 188 Series 1 date questions with an open upper bound resolved above it).` Cost: the date pipeline itself is the work; the prompt sentence is one constant and two presence pins in tests/prompts/. Do NOT add a mechanical floor on the tail mass: my optimum-at-0.40-to-0.537 curve is an n=10 in-sample fit, a floor is a branch the repo's proportion rule disfavours, and the measured win comes entirely from eliminating near-empty tails, which the prompt sentence achieves without a mechanism.
RANK 2 (high impact, low cost): Add the multi-resolution mixture clause, gated on the question's own `multi_resolution` field. Preseason-2 post 650 is live with `multi_resolution: true` and 11 daily bitcoin resolutions, and treating it as a single-instance question costs 38.7 baseline points (12.7 under Mantic's proposed unified coefficient). The gate is the FIELD, not the platform, which is better: read `question.api_json["question"]["multi_resolution"]` (verified reachable, since mantic.py:81 already indexes `question.api_json["question"]["id"]`), and it is simply absent on Metaculus. The clause must NOT interpolate the resolution count, because `resolutions` is null while the question is open and the count lives only in the criteria prose. Recommended constant and text: `# Mantic Series 2 doc §7 'One forecast, many resolutions': the single submitted distribution is scored against every resolution value and the scores averaged, i.e. sum_i (c_i/C)*k*ln(p_i), whose argmax is p_i = E[c_i]/C. So the optimum is the expected EMPIRICAL distribution of the resolution set, a mixture, not the predictive distribution of any one instance. Priced on live post 650 (eleven daily bitcoin closes, 450 bins, 77k spot, 2.5% daily vol): a day-one distribution costs 38.7 baseline points, a terminal-day distribution 4.3, and 12.2 under a 1%/day drift. Gated on the question's own multi_resolution field, which Metaculus does not carry. / _MULTI_RESOLUTION_MIXTURE_RULE = \"\"\"\n               • This question is scored against EVERY resolution value its resolution criteria names, with the\n                 scores averaged, so describe how the quantity is distributed ACROSS those values rather than\n                 where any one of them lands: forecast each instance, then pool those forecasts into a single\n                 mixture and report the mixture's percentiles (a distribution fitted to one instance is scored\n                 as though it had excluded every other value in the set).\"\"\"`. Interpolate it into numeric_prompt, and into stacking_numeric_prompt if a multi-resolution question can reach the stacker. Cost: one constant, three interpolation sites, one presence pin and one absence pin.
RANK 3 (moderate impact, moderate cost, overlaps the numeric-grid agent's slice): Give coarse-grid Mantic quantitative questions a direct per-bin PMF path instead of percentile-to-PCHIP. Preseason-2 post 651 has 12 bins and 3 of them are calendar-impossible; smearing uniformly over 12 instead of 9 costs 14.4 baseline points with zero information, and a confident view smeared across neighbours costs more. This is a representation problem, not a tuning problem: 13 declared percentiles interpolated with PCHIP cannot produce a zero interior bin, and `grid_step_constraints(13)` returns max_step 1.0 so the server is not the constraint, our interpolation is. Suggested shape: when `inbound_outcome_count` is small enough that the grid is enumerable (roughly <= 30), have the forecaster emit a per-bin probability vector the way MC does, and build the CDF from it directly. Flagging rather than specifying because the grid mechanics belong to the numeric-grid agent; my contribution is the 14.4-point price tag and the fact that Mantic will keep producing these.
RANK 4 (low impact, very low cost): Replace the two 'Metaculus peer score' sentences with platform-aware wording, read off `question.page_url`. This is worth doing not for points but because telling a model it is compared to peers invites contrarian drift, which is strictly harmful under a proper score, whereas telling it the reference is a uniform distribution invites nothing. Metaculus text, replacing prompts.py:841 and :1281 (and fixing 'peer' to 'spot peer', which is what those tournaments actually use per performance_analysis/platform_scores.py:4-5): `You will be judged on the accuracy and calibration of your forecast under Metaculus' spot peer log score, which compares you to the other forecasters on this question at a single scoring moment.` Mantic text: `You will be judged on the accuracy and calibration of your forecast under Crucible's spot baseline log score, which compares you to a uniform distribution over the outcomes rather than to other forecasters, so nothing is gained by disagreeing with the obvious answer and nothing is lost by giving it.` Implementation: a leaf helper `question_platform(question)` that reads `urlparse(question.page_url).hostname` against the existing MANTIC_HOST / METACULUS_HOST constants and returns the existing PLATFORM_MANTIC / PLATFORM_METACULUS strings, defaulting to PLATFORM_METACULUS when page_url is None. No prompt-builder signature changes, and it works in stacking.py unchanged. While in there, add one Mantic-only sentence to the numeric prompt's Scoring Rule block replacing the vague 'Probability mass below/above the bounds is scored as a binary event': `Mass beyond an open bound is scored as its own bucket against a fixed 5% reference, so the value of that bucket is set by how far your mass is from 5%, not by how it compares with the inbound bins.` Cost: one helper, four text sites, presence and absence pins per platform in tests/prompts/.
RANK 5 (low impact, low cost, free): Re-run the clip-threshold sweep with a baseline objective before touching the binary clamp, and expect it to say no. `performance_analysis/clip_threshold.py` currently prices candidate floors in spot-peer terms via `spot_peer_delta`; adding a baseline pricing function lets it answer the one question that decides this: among our archived >=0.98 binary calls, are we wrong less than 1 time in 157? That is the exact proper-score crossover for moving the clamp from 0.98 to 0.999. Given that the 2026-09-02 sweep already concluded neither floor moves under peer scoring, that the maximum possible gain is +2.73 points per genuinely-certain question, that the loss at a true 0.98 is -5.93, and that Series 1 had zero binary questions, my prior is strongly that the answer is no. Run it anyway because it is free and offline, and because it settles the question with data rather than an argument.
REFUSE: loosening MC_PROB_MIN for Mantic, and bypassing forecasting-tools' MC validator to do it. The measured expected cost of the 0.01 floor on real Mantic ballots is 0.70 baseline points on average and 6.64 at worst, MC is 5% of the question pool, our clamp-before-construct order already prevents the ValidationError, and forecasting-tools clamps to its own hard-coded 0.01 regardless of what we send, so any real fix means a parallel publish path around the framework's model. That is a mechanism and a branch for roughly 40 points across a 500-question series. The medicine is worse than the disease. Do update the stale sentence in mc_processing.py's docstring that says 'Metaculus MC ballots are far below this cardinality in practice' to note that Mantic runs 50-option ballots, so the next agent does not re-derive this from scratch.
REFUSE: any prompt clause about anticipating resolution rounding or reporting precision. Series 2 doc §5 commits Mantic to aggregating bins UP to whatever precision resolution actually turns out to support and scoring as though the question always had that precision, explicitly so that 'forecasters do not expect to gain by mixing in a second forecast to account for the realised precision.' Our prompts do not currently instruct rounding anticipation, so the correct action is to keep not instructing it. Noting it only so nobody adds it later thinking it is an edge.
OPERATIONAL, not a scoring change but worth more than most of the above: never withdraw a Mantic forecast. Under spot baseline the score reads whichever forecast is active at spot_scoring_time (score_math.py:120-124), Mantic forecasts carry end_time null so ours stays active indefinitely, but a withdrawal sets end_time and would score a hard 0 instead of the 25th-percentile fallback a missing forecast receives. Combined with the 27.1-point measured cost of missing a question and the one-hour forecasting window, this makes submit-something-always the single highest-value operational rule on this platform.
