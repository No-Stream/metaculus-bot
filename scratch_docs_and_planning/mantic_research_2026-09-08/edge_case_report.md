## Mantic (Crucible) readiness review: 25 verified findings, ranked by expected cost

Grounding for a reader with no context. The repository at /Users/flatljan/personal/metaculus-bot is a forecasting bot that reads open questions from a tournament API, runs three frontier language models over researched evidence, aggregates their forecasts and publishes a prediction plus a comment. Branch `mantic-competition` adds a second platform: Mantic's "Crucible" bot-only tournament at competitions.mantic.com, which runs on a fork of the open-source Metaculus code, pays $3 per forecast, and currently exposes a four-question trial tournament ("Preseason 2", slug `preseason-2`) that stops accepting forecasts on 2026-09-20. Series 2 follows on an unannounced date under a slug that does not exist yet. Five hunter agents returned 55 raw findings; I deduplicated them into 25, verified each one myself against the code, the Series 2 rules document, the Metaculus backend source, or free unauthenticated GETs to Mantic's public API, and dropped ten claims that did not survive. Two verification artefacts are worth naming because several rankings rest on them: I crawled all 556 public Mantic posts (520 Series 1, 32 practice, 4 preseason) into /tmp/mantic_corpus.json, and I confirmed Mantic's out-of-range scoring formula by matching `50*ln(tail/0.05)` against the platform's own reported `spot_baseline_score` on 146 of 146 resolved out-of-range questions with zero error.

**1. Out-of-range tail mass is structurally 1% while Mantic scores that bucket against 5%.** When all three ensemble members declare their thirteen percentiles inside a question's displayed range, which the repo's own performance doc says is the normal case (`docs/performance_analysis.md:579-583`: the quantity 1 minus F(p99) "is about 0.01 on every record by construction"), the published cumulative distribution puts exactly 1.00% above the ceiling. I reproduced that exact 0.010000 on the recorded 451-bin preseason bitcoin question. Mantic scores an out-of-range resolution as 50 times the natural log of that mass divided by 0.05, which I verified to zero error against the platform's own reported scores on 146 of 146 such questions. So 1% is -80.5 baseline points where 5% is 0 and where simply not forecasting earns the field's 25th percentile. This matters because Mantic escapes its own ranges far more often than Metaculus does: I computed 22.3% of resolved discrete questions, 6.6% of numeric, 15.0% combined and 51.0% of date questions, against 2.2 to 2.6% in the repo's Metaculus archives. Recommended handling is two cheap steps and one deliberate hold. Add the measured base rate to the existing bound messages in the numeric prompt, mode-gated so Metaculus behaviour cannot move, and land the out-of-range mass marker from item 8 in the same merge. Hold the mechanical floor: moving each side from 0.01 to 0.05 gains 80.5 points when the outcome escapes and costs 4.26 when it does not, which breaks even at a 5.03% escape rate against Mantic's measured 15% and 51%, so it is probably right, but it would override an honest forecaster and would be inert if the models already place percentiles beyond the bound, and only the telemetry can tell us which.

**2. GitHub is currently delivering about a fifth of this repository's scheduled runs.** I queried the GitHub API for the three-cron Metaculus tournament workflow, which should fire 72 times a day: it delivered 63 and 67 runs on 2026-08-24 and 08-25, then 7, 18, 18, 19, 22 and 16 through 2026-09-07. On 2026-09-07 the sixteen delivered start times leave eleven of twenty-four hours with no run at all and include gaps of three and a half hours. The Metaculus Cup workflow, also three crons, shows the same 17 to 23 per day, so this is repository-wide rather than workflow-specific and outside our control. It matters because I recomputed from Mantic's API that every one of the 520 Series 1 questions opened at minute zero second zero with a window of exactly 60.0 minutes and none closed early, and the Mantic workflow has only two cron entries, at :17 and :47. If Series 2 restores that cadence, roughly half of all questions would never be fetched while open, each scored at the 25th percentile with its $3 forgone, and the top-95% safety net forgives only 5%. Recommended handling is free and immediate: replace the two entries with six at :05, :15, :25, :35, :45 and :55, all unused by the other bot workflows so the distinct-minute test stays green, and a delivered run with nothing new to forecast spends nothing. Be honest about the ceiling, though: the delivered runs cluster inside good hours (16:23 with 16:31, 20:28 with 20:38, 23:25 with 23:34), so extra entries buy less than independent drops would imply and cannot cover a three-hour blackout. Before Series 2 opens, the operator needs to choose between one job that keeps re-polling to the end of its hour and a workflow_dispatch driven from an always-on machine, since dispatch events are not subject to schedule dropping.

**3. A forecast that is almost entirely outside an open bound raises on a floating-point epsilon.** I reproduced this on the recorded preseason question: a declaration putting essentially all mass above the ceiling reaches the last repair tier in `metaculus_bot/numeric/pchip_cdf.py`, whose trigger at line 664 fires on a step deficit of 1.17e-18 and which then refuses at line 529 because the required range exceeds the available range by 8.67e-17. Both comparisons are untoleranced, while the assertion at line 575 and the post-check at line 552 in the same file already allow `min_step - 1e-10`. The result is that the member's whole numeric forecast is discarded, and because the trigger is a property of the question rather than of one model, agreement across the ensemble means all three fail together and the question publishes nothing after paying for a full research bundle and fan-out. This matters most on the shape that scores best here: a 0.98 out-of-range mass is worth +148.8 baseline points. Recommended handling is to give the trigger at line 664 the same 1e-10 tolerance the file already uses elsewhere, and the raise at line 529 the same for defence in depth. I verified this works: with that one change, every failing shape I tried (15, 200, 450 and 2000 bins, with open and closed floors, including the 201-point case that was failing through the forecasting-tools fallback with its own hardcoded step) builds a valid distribution carrying 0.98 to 0.99 out-of-range mass. It is a raise turned into a publish, with no new branch, flag or fallback, and it should ship with the three reproductions as tests.

**4. A bracketed API query URL is truncated and its unfiltered answer is served as grading evidence.** Mantic question writers frequently resolve a question to "the count returned by this API query" and cite the query URL, which for the Federal Register looks like `.../documents.json?conditions[agencies][]=nuclear-regulatory-commission&...`. The URL character class at `metaculus_bot/research/resolution_url_scan.py:48` excludes the closing bracket but allows the opening one, and there is a balanced-parentheses atom with no balanced-bracket equivalent, so the match stops at the first bracket. I ran the repo's own extractor over the resolution text of all 556 Mantic posts and got six such truncated URLs, on posts 349, 434, 459, 387, 145 and 598. Then I probed both forms: the truncated Federal Register query returns HTTP 200 with `{"description":"All Documents","count":10000}` while the correct query returns `count: 125`. The resolution-source fetcher is enabled in the Mantic workflow, so that body lands in the research bundle under the "## Resolution Source Snapshot" heading as primary grading evidence, eighty times wrong, on a question whose upper bound is 75. This is the worst failure class in the review after nothing: a forecast that looks sourced and is wrong. Recommended handling is a balanced-bracket atom mirroring the existing balanced-parentheses one, so `conditions[agencies][]=` stays inside the URL while a lone trailing bracket from prose still ends the match, with a test built from the real resolution text of posts 434 and 598. Ship the trailing-backtick fix in the same edit: I found ten backtick-terminated URLs across nine posts, and confirmed that the backticked openFDA endpoint returns 404 where the clean form returns 200.

**5. When the preseason closes, the Mantic runs go green and silent for two weeks.** `MANTIC_TOURNAMENT_ID` is pinned to `preseason-2` with an end date of 2026-09-20, and `TOURNAMENT_HARD_STOP_WEEKS` is 2 and is shared with the Metaculus modes, so `check_tournament_dates` only logs a warning for fourteen days before it raises. I confirmed that a zero-question run is green end to end: an empty report list raises nothing in the log summary, every degradation counter stays at zero, and the exit condition in `cli` is satisfied. I also confirmed by public GET that an ended-but-existing Mantic tournament answers HTTP 200 with an empty results list rather than an error, while an unknown slug answers HTTP 400. So if Series 2 opens under a new slug before somebody repoints the constant, every scheduled run keeps fetching the dead preseason, publishes nothing, and reports success, for up to two weeks. At Series 1's realised rate that is roughly seventy questions and $210 of forgone payments, discovered only when the hard stop fires. Recommended handling reuses a pattern already in the file: `check_fall_cup_reminder` logs loudly at startup and returns a boolean that the degradation reporter turns into a non-zero exit after publishing. Thread the same shape through the Mantic branch so a date past the Mantic end date exits red within a day. One boolean and one condition, no new mechanism, and a genuinely quiet hour still exits green. Do not cut the shared hard-stop constant.

**6. The numeric prompt describes a different platform's scoring rule, on the axis that matters most.** The Scoring Rule paragraph at `metaculus_bot/prompts.py:1154`, repeated in the stacker prompt at 1498, tells the model that Metaculus continuous questions add a uniform 0.01 floor to every probability density so excluding the truth costs about ln(0.01) = -4.6, and that sharpness above about 35 does not pay. On Mantic the leaderboard is baseline points, there is no uniform density floor, the per-bin cap scales with the grid rather than being a constant, and an out-of-range resolution is measured against a 5% reference with no floor, so a 1% tail is -80.5 points today and the Series 2 change makes it either much worse or differently scaled. The prompt therefore tells the model the cliff is more than an order of magnitude shallower than it is, which works directly against the item-1 fix. The prompts also address the model with "Your Metaculus question is" and name Metaculus' peer score on a platform where peer scoring has been replaced and no community prediction exists during the open window. Recommended handling is a deletion rather than an addition: cut the two false sentences and the platform attribution, keep the proper-scoring-rule sentence which is true everywhere, and add one sentence saying that mass beyond an open bound is scored as its own outcome against a reference of a few percent so starving it is heavily punished. Ship it with the item-1 base-rate clause and the item-9 rewrite in one prompt edit, with the presence and absence pins the repo's prompt tests require. Do not try to reproduce the Series 2 formula: it is not yet observable because no Series 2 question has resolved.

**7. A concentrated forecast on a low-cardinality discrete grid publishes flattened.** Half of Mantic's quantitative questions are discrete (159 of 315), 132 of them with a grid step of exactly 1.0, and eighteen have thirteen bins or fewer, against about 8% discrete in the repo's Metaculus archives. The prompt requires thirteen strictly increasing percentile values, which no integer sequence can supply on a three-bin grid, so the model repeats integers. The cluster spreader then treats the plateau as a formatting problem and spreads it a full unit per position, because `COUNT_LIKE_DELTA_MULTIPLIER` is 1.0 whenever the values are integer-flavoured, which is always true on an integer-centred grid, and the distribution is read at bin edges. I ran real corpus questions through the repo's own pipeline: on post 253 (three bins) a declaration meaning 90% on the first bin publishes [0.5584, 0.4121, 0.0295]; on post 430 (four bins) 80% publishes 0.4965; on post 643 (twenty-one bins) 70% publishes 0.4337 with 0.4302 on the neighbour. The machinery is capable of publishing faithfully: expressing the same intent as fractional values inside the intended bin publishes 0.8929. On post 253 the loss is 24 baseline points under today's formula and about 43 under the Series 2 log-base-N form, on a question where each bin carries the most score. Recommended handling is to cap the count-like spread by the grid rather than by a fixed unit, taking the spread as the smaller of 1.0 and the bin width and capping the total plateau spread at one bin width, which is a change to an existing formula rather than a new branch, paired with one templated prompt sentence naming the bin width and bin count and telling the model that a percentile's value selects the bin it falls in. Both touch the shared numeric build, so they belong in one merge before the season's first question and are a configuration-era boundary for the Metaculus discrete slice too.

**8. Nothing records the published out-of-range mass, so the biggest lever cannot be measured.** The per-member forecast marker records what the extraction ladder read and what the runner handed on, but not the built distribution or the question's bounds; the Mantic question marker records the grid size but not the bounds; and the existing open-bound-piling diagnostic measures terminal-bin mass, a different quantity, and only when nothing was declared beyond the edge. I confirmed the two states are indistinguishable in the logs by producing both on the same question: 0.010000 above the ceiling when every percentile sits inside, and 0.98 when they do not. That means a residual round cannot answer "did we starve the out-of-range bucket on the questions that escaped their range" without re-deriving every member's distribution from its declared percentiles plus a fresh API read of each question's bounds, and after ninety days the GitHub Actions logs and their inputs are gone. The repo's own standing rule is that a new signal without a marker specification is invisible after ninety days. Recommended handling is two additive fields, the low and high out-of-range masses, on the existing per-question numeric telemetry for both each member and the published aggregate, appended at the end of the line so the registry's positional pattern keeps matching, with the register entry and a verbatim example line in the marker tests. Additive only, never renaming or re-spelling an existing field. This is the instrument that makes the item-1 floor decision cheap after the first live runs.

**9. The prompt tells the model that a candidate far outside the displayed range is probably the wrong series variant.** The clause lives at `metaculus_bot/prompts.py:789-793` and reaches every Mantic quantitative question, including discrete ones, because the discrete question class subclasses the numeric one and uses the numeric prompt. Its stated reason is that the bounds were set by someone who could see the real series. That is true on Metaculus and false on Mantic, where the Series 2 rules document confirms that question writers are paid for bot disagreement and are moving to top-k disagreement scoring with a separate quantitative prize, and where I measured that 22.3% of resolved discrete and 51.0% of date questions escaped their range. So on a count question whose ceiling is deliberately too low, a forecaster that extrapolates correctly is then told by our own prompt that it has probably picked the wrong variant, and pulls its percentiles back inside. That swings the score from roughly +115 to -80.5, about 195 baseline points, on the exact family Mantic's leaderboard rewards. Recommended handling keeps the measured content and drops the false premise: say that the displayed range is weak evidence about which series variant resolves and is not evidence about the magnitude of the outcome. That preserves the original correction (do not read inside-the-range as confirming the headline series) in a form that is true on both platforms, and it is a deletion rather than an added branch. Ship it with the other prompt edits, and do not add a Mantic-only prompt variant.

**10. Mantic forfeits leave no trace, so the cadence decision cannot be settled on evidence.** The tournament fetch asks only for open questions, so a question that opened and closed between two delivered runs is simply absent from the response rather than visibly skipped, and there is no counter, marker or log line for it. The tool built for exactly this question on Metaculus, `scripts/supply_probe.py`, is Metaculus-only: it imports the Metaculus client and the Metaculus identity preflight and takes Metaculus slugs. That blocks the item-2 decision, because choosing between six crons, an in-run poll loop and an external trigger turns on the forfeit rate, and it also blocks any per-round residual read on Mantic, where the forfeit population is likely the dominant score term. The measurement is free and needs no credential: I crawled all 556 posts unauthenticated in a couple of minutes, and resolved posts expose every competitor's full distribution and per-question scores. Recommended handling is a Mantic mode for the existing supply probe: page the Mantic tournament for closed and resolved posts, count how many carry a forecast from the bot's user id, and report the miss rate per release hour. Read-only, free, no production path touched, and it reuses the probe's existing paging and reporting. It is the cheapest instrument for the most expensive failure mode in this review.

### Accepted with no change

- High-cardinality multiple-choice ballots are ceiling-limited at 1 minus 0.01 times (n-1), which caps a 51-option favourite at 0.50; publishing the honest number means bypassing the forecasting-tools validator for about fifteen points on half a percent of questions.
- The four-worker publish pool cannot starve a future at Mantic's concurrency, since the most questions ever open at once was three.
- Mantic never closed a Series 1 question early (0 of 520), so reading only the scheduled close time is correct; the single early close is from a hand-edited practice tournament.
- Clock and timezone handling is sound: budgets are taken once from a timezone-aware clock and elapsed time is monotonic, against a measured server skew under one second.
- Upcoming, group, conditional and notebook posts are shapes Mantic does not produce (all 556 corpus posts carry only a question key); the two-line defensive read in the Mantic client is folded into item 12 instead.
- Rate limiting, revoked tokens and 400, 404 and 405 publish responses all end in a red run carrying the platform's own diagnostic text, and the host has no throttle source.
- Resolution-time bin aggregation leaves the optimal submission unchanged, so nothing should reason about `precision` or `date_granularity` for scoring and adjacent-bin hedging must not be added.
- The one-question-per-post comment failure and the absent Mantic comment-backfill record class cost no forecasts; they get one sentence in the performance-analysis doc, not a retry path.
- The 70-minute run-step cap exceeding Mantic's window is bounded and rare, and tightening it means editing two pinned tests whose rationale is the Metaculus window.
- The unit-mismatch guard's one-sidedness cannot be fixed symmetrically, because a nearly-all-out-of-range declaration is the correct answer on a quarter of discrete and half of date questions.
- Series 2's preseason discrete question puts its nominal ceiling half a step above the top bin centre; the offset is a fraction of one bin on an open bound and the repo correctly prefers the server's own nominal fields.
- Date questions are 37.6% of the corpus and 51.0% of them resolved above the ceiling, which is the parallel workstream's business; the three facts worth handing over are that the out-of-range bucket carries most of the probability on the modal date question, that daily bins on a long window can approach the 2,000-bin ceiling, and that the preseason's own date question has both bounds closed so both shapes must work.
